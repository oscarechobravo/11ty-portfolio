I am thinking about personal journeys and personal growth (in the expansion of mind, body and soul) as I write about traditional views of change in computing. I am specifically thinking about how 11 years ago I wrote about the need to do more holistic/systemic work to understand the breadth and depth of ICT's emissions and it's relationship with everyday life. 

I am now older, wiser (?), and ready to admit that back then I couldn't fathom the system, that I exist in and was trying to understand, dying or being put to rest. I feel like I am centring my lived experience way more than I was back then, and allowing myself to be conscious of the decay of modernity in ways that I couldn't see before. 

It's tough to reckon with a complete change in world view, but I strongly believe that we need to be thinking more about hospicing and endings in the work we do. And of course that comes with a radical rethinking of how we grieve and do endings. There are no complete thoughts right now, it's just something I've been thinking about as I work with friends on the Berkana Two Loops and what that means for our lives and practice. 

Elina reminds me of Chefurka's Ladder of Awareness.

> Chefurka’s Ladder of Awareness:
> 1.Dead asleep.
> 2.Awareness of one fundamental problem. 
> 3.Awareness of many problems.
> 4.Awareness of the interconnections between the many problems.
> 5.Awareness that the predicament encompasses all aspects of life.

In 2014 I was at 2 or maybe 3, and now I am at 5, and I think it's time to let the system die, gracefully of course.
